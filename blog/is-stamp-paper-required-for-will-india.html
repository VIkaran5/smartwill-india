Is Stamp Paper Required for a Will in India? (The Plain A4 Paper Rule)

Does your Will need a ₹100 or ₹500 non-judicial stamp paper? Under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, a Will is completely exempt from stamp duty. Here is why plain A4 paper is 100% legally enforceable across India.

Indian Stamp Act, 1899 6 min read Updated September 2026 SmartWill Legal Desk

The Short Legal Answer: NO. Under the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, a Will is completely exempt from stamp duty. There is absolutely no requirement to use ₹100, ₹500, or any other denomination of non-judicial stamp paper. A Will executed on a plain white A4 paper is 100% legally enforceable across India.

If you ask an average Indian whether stamp paper is required for a Will in India, chances are they will say yes. The myth that legal documents are only valid if printed on green non-judicial stamp paper is deeply ingrained in Indian society. This misconception is often perpetuated by touts and notary agents outside courts who profit from selling stamp papers at a premium.

This misunderstanding prevents many families from securing their assets. They assume that creating a Will is a costly, bureaucratic hassle. The truth, however, is that Indian law makes executing a Will remarkably simple and entirely free from stamp duty.

1. The #1 Myth: "Wills Need ₹100/₹500 Stamp Paper"

Many people confuse Wills with other property documents like Sale Deeds, Gift Deeds, or Lease Agreements. For those documents, the Indian Stamp Act mandates payment of ad valorem stamp duty based on the property's value. Without the correct stamp paper, a Sale Deed is invalid and inadmissible in court.

However, a Will is fundamentally different. A Will is a testamentary document that only takes effect after the death of the testator (the person making the Will). Because it can be revoked or changed at any time during the testator's life, the law does not treat it as an inter-vivos (living person to living person) transfer of property.

2. Section 18 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899: Exemption from Stamp Duty

The Indian Stamp Act, 1899 governs the stamp duty payable on various legal instruments in India. Schedule I of the Act lists all documents that require stamp paper and specifies the applicable duty.

If you examine Schedule I of the Indian Stamp Act (and the corresponding State Stamp Acts), you will find that Wills are conspicuously absent from the list of documents requiring stamp duty. Because a Will is not explicitly listed as an instrument chargeable with duty, it is exempt by default.

"Legal Precedent: The Supreme Court of India has repeatedly held that a Will, by its very nature as a revocable instrument taking effect post-mortem, does not attract stamp duty under the Indian Stamp Act. A plain paper Will is fully admissible and enforceable in courts."

3. Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925: Valid Execution on Plain A4 Paper

So, if stamp paper is not required, what does the law actually demand? The requirements for a valid Will are governed by Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925. The criteria are surprisingly straightforward:

  • Any Plain Paper: The Will can be written, typed, or printed on any clean paper. Most legal practitioners recommend standard A4 size plain white paper for clarity and durability.
  • Testator's Signature: The person making the Will must sign or affix their thumb impression on the document, ideally on every page to prevent tampering.
  • Two Independent Witnesses: The signature must be witnessed by at least two independent people who are present at the same time and who also sign the document. These witnesses must not be beneficiaries in the Will.

Nowhere in Section 63 does it mention stamp paper, legal paper, green sheets, or any specific stationery.

4. Why Predatory Touts and Notary Agents Push Unnecessary Stamp Papers

If the law is so clear, why does the myth persist? The answer lies in the informal legal economy outside many Indian courts and Sub-Registrar offices.

Many notary agents and touts earn a living by selling stamp paper and providing drafting services. When an uninformed citizen approaches them to draft a Will, the agent will often insist on using a ₹100 or ₹500 stamp paper. This allows them to charge a markup on the stamp paper and inflate the perceived value of their drafting services. They convince the citizen that the green paper makes the document "more official."

5. State-Wise Reality Check: No State Requires Stamp Paper

Stamp duty is a concurrent subject in India, meaning states have their own Stamp Acts (e.g., the Maharashtra Stamp Act, Karnataka Stamp Act). However, the rule regarding Wills is uniform across the country:

  • Maharashtra: No stamp paper required for a Will.
  • Karnataka: No stamp paper required for a Will.
  • Delhi: No stamp paper required for a Will.
  • Telangana & Andhra Pradesh: No stamp paper required for a Will.
  • Tamil Nadu: No stamp paper required for a Will.

Across all 28 states and 8 Union Territories, the exemption of Wills from stamp duty holds true.

6. What About Notarization? (Optional, Not Required)

Another common question is whether a plain paper Will needs to be notarized by a Notary Public. Similar to stamp paper and registration, notarization is entirely optional.

While having a Notary attest your signature provides an additional layer of witness verification, it does not magically increase the legal validity of the Will. A plain paper Will attested by two ordinary witnesses (like friends, neighbors, or a family doctor) is just as valid as a notarized one. If you later choose to register your document for archival protection, see our guide on how to register a Will at the Sub-Registrar Office.

7. Step-by-Step: How to Execute a Valid Will on Plain A4 Paper

To ensure your plain paper Will is bulletproof in court, follow these simple steps:

  1. Draft Clearly: Use unambiguous language to describe your assets and beneficiaries. You can use platforms like SmartWill India to generate a legally sound draft online.
  2. Print on A4 Paper: Print the final draft on standard white A4 paper. Make sure the print is clear and legible.
  3. Gather Witnesses: Invite two reliable, independent adults who are not receiving any property under the Will to act as witnesses.
  4. Sign in Presence of Witnesses: Sign your name or affix your thumbprint at the bottom of every page and at the very end of the document. Both witnesses must physically watch you do this.
  5. Witness Signatures: Immediately after you sign, both witnesses must sign the last page in your presence, providing their names, addresses, and ID details (like Aadhaar numbers).
  6. Store Safely: Keep the original signed document in a secure location (like a bank locker) and inform your executor or trusted family members of its whereabouts.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a Will valid on a plain white paper in India?

Yes, absolutely. Under Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, a Will typed or handwritten on plain A4 white paper is 100% legally valid as long as it is signed by the testator in the presence of two independent witnesses who also sign in each other's presence.

Is stamp paper required for a Will in India?

No. Under Schedule I of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899, testamentary instruments like Wills are completely exempt from stamp duty. There is zero legal requirement to execute a Will on a ₹100, ₹500, or any stamp paper.

Does an unregistered plain paper Will hold value in court?

Yes. Under Section 18 of the Indian Registration Act, 1908, Will registration is strictly optional. An unregistered plain paper Will holds full legal evidentiary weight in courts for obtaining probate, legal heir certificates, or transferring bank accounts and properties.

Can an executor or beneficiary sign as a witness on plain paper?

An executor can sign as a witness if they receive no personal bequest under the Will. However, under Section 67 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, any bequest or legacy given to an attesting witness (or their spouse) becomes null and void. Therefore, beneficiaries must NEVER sign as witnesses.
