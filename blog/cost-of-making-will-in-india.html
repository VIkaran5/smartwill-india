2. Hidden Costs in Traditional Will Drafting

When you hire a traditional lawyer to draft your Will, the upfront quote (e.g., ₹10,000) often isn't the final amount you pay. There are numerous hidden costs built into the offline legal system:

  • Stamp Paper Touts: Even though a Will does not require stamp paper in India, many traditional lawyers and local touts will advise you to execute it on ₹100 or ₹500 non-judicial stamp paper "just to be safe." This is an unnecessary cost and often requires paying a premium to a stamp vendor.
  • Notary Charges: Similarly, lawyers often recommend getting the Will notarized. While helpful as secondary evidence, notarization is not legally required under Indian law and adds extra fees and time.
  • Revision Fees: If you buy a new car, open a new bank account, or have another child three years from now, you will need to update your Will. A traditional lawyer will typically charge you a "revision fee" (often 50% of the original drafting cost) just to make minor edits to the document.
  • Registration "Assistance": While registering a Will is optional, many lawyers will offer to "handle the registration" at the Sub-Registrar's office for an additional ₹5,000 to ₹15,000, framing it as a mandatory final step.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a lawyer charge for a Will in India?

Lawyer fees for drafting a Will in India typically range from ₹5,000 for standard city lawyers to upwards of ₹15,000-₹50,000 for High Court advocates and specialized estate planning firms, depending on the complexity of your assets and family structure.

Are online Wills legally valid in India?

Yes, online Wills are entirely legally valid in India. The medium of drafting (online software vs manual typing) does not matter. The only legal requirement under Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925 is that the final document must be printed on paper, signed by the testator, and attested by two independent witnesses.
