Is Registration of a Will Mandatory in India? (Section 18 Explained)

By SmartWill India Legal Desk  ·  Updated September 2026  ·  5 min read

The Short Legal Answer: NO. Under Section 18(e) of the Indian Registration Act, 1908, the registration of a Will is completely optional in India. A Will typed on plain A4 paper, signed by the testator, and attested by two witnesses is 100% legally enforceable in Indian courts.

One of the most persistent myths surrounding estate planning in India is that you must hire an advocate, purchase expensive non-judicial stamp paper, and visit the Sub-Registrar's office to stand in long queues for your Will to be legally recognized.

This misconception causes thousands of Indians to postpone making a Will indefinitely, leaving their families vulnerable to frozen bank accounts and bitter civil disputes. In this guide, we review the exact statutory provisions under Indian law.

1. The Law: Indian Registration Act, 1908

Section 17 of the Indian Registration Act, 1908 lists all documents whose registration is compulsory (such as sale deeds, gift deeds of immovable property, and leases exceeding one year).

Wills are deliberately excluded from Section 17. Instead, Wills appear under Section 18(e), which explicitly lists documents for which registration is optional:

"Section 18: Documents of which registration is optional — Any of the following documents may be registered under this Act, namely: ... (e) Wills;"

Because registration is optional, an unregistered Will carries the exact same legal weight in probate and succession matters as a registered Will, provided the formal requirements under the Indian Succession Act, 1925 are satisfied.

2. What Actually Makes a Will Legally Valid in India?

Under Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925, only three simple conditions are required for a valid Will:

  1. Written Document: It can be typed or handwritten in any language (English, Telugu, Hindi, etc.) on plain A4 paper.
  2. Testator's Signature / Thumb Impression: The person making the Will (testator) must sign or affix their thumb impression on every page of the document while in sound disposing mind.
  3. Two Independent Witnesses: At least two witnesses must see the testator sign the document, and must sign the witness section in the presence of the testator. (Important: Witnesses should not be beneficiaries named in the Will).

4. Does a Plain Paper Will Need Notarization?

No. Notarization is also not legally mandatory. While having a Notary Public stamp your Will provides secondary verification that you signed in their presence, lack of notarization does not invalidate the Will. The primary requirement is always the attestation of two independent witnesses.

5. When Should You Consider Registering Your Will?

You may consider visiting the Sub-Registrar's office if:

  • You anticipate bitter disputes between family members where one party may falsely allege forgery or undue influence.
  • You are disinheriting immediate Class 1 legal heirs in favor of a non-relative or charity.
  • You want a government-backed record stored in official archives in case the original document is lost or destroyed.

Even in such cases, having a clean, well-drafted Will on plain paper executed today is infinitely better than waiting months or years for a Sub-Registrar appointment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I write a Will on plain A4 paper?

Yes. Any paper (ruled or unruled, printed or handwritten) is legally valid in India under the Indian Succession Act 1925. No legal paper (green sheet) or stamp paper is mandatory.

Can a beneficiary be a witness?

No. Section 67 of the Indian Succession Act states that any bequest or gift made to an attesting witness is void. Always choose two neutral witnesses (friends, colleagues, doctors, neighbors) who receive nothing under the Will.

What happens if I make a new Will later?

Under Section 62 of the Indian Succession Act, every Will can be revoked or modified at any time by the testator during their lifetime. The latest dated, properly executed Will automatically revokes all prior Wills.
