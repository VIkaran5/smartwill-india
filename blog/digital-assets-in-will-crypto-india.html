Digital Assets & Crypto in Your Will in India: Complete Guide (2026)

September 29, 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  Verified by SmartWill Legal Desk

Today, a significant portion of our lives and wealth exists entirely in the digital realm: cryptocurrency portfolios, demat share accounts, domain names, YouTube channels, cloud photo archives, and SaaS business subscriptions.

Yet, when an individual passes away in India without a digital succession plan, billions of rupees in cryptocurrencies and irrecoverable family memories become permanently locked behind two-factor authentication (2FA) and encryption keys.

The Modern Dilemma: Traditional Wills written decades ago only considered physical land, cash, and gold. How do you bequeath cryptocurrencies and digital accounts under Indian succession law without violating privacy or cybersecurity regulations?

What Qualifies as a "Digital Asset" in India?

Under the Information Technology Act, 2000 and the Finance Act, 2022, digital assets broadly fall into three categories:

  1. Financial Digital Assets: Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs) such as Bitcoin, Ethereum, and NFTs; balances on crypto exchanges (CoinDCX, CoinSwitch, Binance, WazirX); digital wallets (Paytm, PhonePe); and reward points.
  2. Digital Business & Intellectual Property: Website domain names, software codebases (GitHub), YouTube creator channels, affiliate accounts, and monetized blogs.
  3. Personal Digital Footprint: Cloud storage (Google Drive, Apple iCloud), email archives (Gmail, Outlook), social media accounts, and digital family photos.

The Golden Rule: Never Put Passwords in a Will

Critical Security Best Practice: Never write private keys, recovery seed phrases, passwords, or PINs inside your Will. Why? A Will may be read by attesting witnesses, viewed by registrar clerks, or submitted to courts during probate where it becomes a public record!

Instead, use the Two-Document System:

  • Document 1 (The Legal Will): Declares ownership of the assets and legally names who inherits them (e.g., "I bequeath all my cryptocurrency holdings stored on CoinDCX and cold storage hardware wallets to my wife, Anita.").
  • Document 2 (The Confidential Digital Access Letter): A private, encrypted document stored in a password manager (like Bitwarden or 1Password) or safe locker, containing instructions on how the executor can retrieve the keys post-demise.

Empowering the Digital Executor

In your SmartWill, you can grant your executor explicit powers to handle digital data:

  • Authority to access, download, or delete personal digital accounts under the Information Technology Act 2000.
  • Power to liquidate or transfer cryptocurrencies and digital investments to designated bank accounts.
  • Right to permanently delete or archive sensitive personal files and emails.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is cryptocurrency taxable when inherited through a Will in India?

Under Section 56(2)(x) of the Income Tax Act 1961, assets (including VDAs and cryptocurrencies) received under a Will or by way of inheritance are completely exempt from income tax at the time of receipt. Capital gains tax only applies when the beneficiary eventually sells the crypto.

2. What happens to a deceased person's WhatsApp and Instagram accounts?

Meta permits immediate family members or the legal executor to request memorialization or permanent deletion of the deceased's account upon submitting a death certificate.
