1. The Legal Doctrine: Why NRIs Need a Specific Indian Will

Many Non-Resident Indians mistakenly assume that a Will executed under local law in Canada, the United States, or the United Kingdom automatically governs their ancestral homes, agricultural lands, or flats in Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Mumbai, or Delhi.

Under international private law and the lex situs doctrine (the law where property is located), the succession of immovable property situated in India is strictly governed by the substantive law of India — specifically the Indian Succession Act, 1925.

Statutory Fact: A foreign Will (probated in Ontario, California, or London) cannot be directly executed in Indian Land Revenue Records or Sub-Registrar offices without undergoing a costly and lengthy re-sealing or Indian High Court probate process. Having a separate, clean Indian Will eliminates years of legal delays.

2. Statutory Execution under Section 63: Can You Sign Outside India?

Yes, 100% legally valid. Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act 1925 defines the mandatory requirements for executing an unprivileged Will:

  1. Testator's Signature: The testator (you) must sign or affix your mark to the Will document.
  2. Two Competent Witnesses: The Will must be attested by two or more witnesses, each of whom must see the testator sign the Will in their presence.
  3. Physical Presence in India is NOT Mandatory: Nowhere does Indian law state that the testator or witnesses must be located on Indian soil at the moment of execution.

This means you can print your SmartWill document at home in Toronto, Dallas, London, or Dubai, sign it at your dining table in front of two friends or colleagues who act as witnesses, and the document is immediately legally binding under Indian law.

3. Who Can Act as Witnesses in a Foreign Country?

One of the most common questions diaspora Indians ask is whether their witnesses must be Indian passport holders.

Under Indian law, any individual of sound mind who has attained the age of majority (18 years) can act as an attesting witness. They can be foreign citizens, permanent residents, or fellow NRIs.

CRITICAL RULE: The two witnesses must not be beneficiaries under the Will (nor spouses of beneficiaries). Having a beneficiary sign as a witness invalidates their bequest under Section 67 of the Indian Succession Act 1925. Choose independent friends, neighbors, or professional colleagues.

4. Indian Consulate Notarization & Apostille (Is it Mandatory?)

Under Section 18 of the Indian Registration Act 1908, registering a Will in India is completely optional. An unregistered Will signed in front of 2 witnesses is legally valid and executable in Indian civil courts.

However, when executing a Will outside India, estate planning experts recommend the following best practices:

  • Local Notary Public: Have the testator and the two witnesses sign before a local Notary Public in your city (Toronto, New York, London, etc.). The Notary adds an official stamp and seal.
  • Indian Consulate / Embassy Attestation: You can optionally visit or mail the document to the nearest Indian High Commission / Consulate General for consular attestation under the Diplomatic and Consular Officers (Oaths and Fees) Act. This provides undeniable proof of execution if the Will is ever challenged by distant relatives in India.

5. FEMA & RBI Rules: What Can NRIs Inherit and Bequeath?

Under the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-debt Instruments) Rules and RBI Master Directions:

  • Residential & Commercial Real Estate: NRIs and OCIs can freely inherit, hold, gift, or transfer residential flats and commercial properties in India without requiring prior Reserve Bank of India clearance.
  • Agricultural Land & Farm Houses: While NRIs cannot directly purchase agricultural land in India, they can legally inherit agricultural land, farmhouses, and plantation properties from Indian residents through a Will.
  • Movable Assets (NRE/NRO/Demat): You can bequeath funds in NRE/NRO fixed deposits, Indian mutual funds, Demat accounts, and physical gold to any resident or non-resident legal heir.

8. Frequently Asked Questions by NRIs

Can an NRI make a Will for properties located in India while living abroad?

Yes. Under Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act 1925, any person of sound mind who owns property in India can execute an Indian Will from abroad. Physical presence in India is NOT legally required. You can sign the printed Will outside India in the presence of two independent adult witnesses.

Can foreign citizens or friends abroad act as witnesses for an Indian Will?

Yes. Indian succession law specifies that any two adults of sound mind who are not beneficiaries under the Will can act as attesting witnesses. They do not have to be Indian citizens or physically located in India.

Is registration or Indian Embassy notarization mandatory for an NRI Will?

Under Section 18 of the Indian Registration Act 1908, Will registration is optional. An unregistered Will signed in front of 2 witnesses is 100% legally binding. However, getting it notarized locally or attested at the Indian Embassy adds strong evidentiary weight in Indian High Court probate proceedings.

Can NRIs inherit agricultural land and residential property in India under FEMA?

Yes. Under RBI and FEMA regulations, NRIs and OCIs can legally inherit any immovable property in India, including residential flats, commercial properties, and agricultural land, without needing prior approval from the Reserve Bank of India.

Should an NRI have separate Wills for foreign assets and Indian assets?

Yes, legal experts strongly advise maintaining separate concurrent Wills: one domestic Will drafted under local law (e.g. Canadian or US law) for assets located abroad, and a dedicated Indian Will drafted under the Indian Succession Act 1925 for assets located in India.
