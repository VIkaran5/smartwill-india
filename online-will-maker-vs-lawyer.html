What Makes a Will Legally Valid in India?

To understand why an online Will holds identical legal weight to a lawyer-drafted Will, you only need to examine Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act, 1925.

Under Section 63, Indian law imposes exactly three statutory requirements for an unprivileged Will:

  1. Written Form: The document must be in writing (handwritten or typed).
  2. Testator's Signature: The testator must sign or affix their thumb impression, intending to give effect to the writing as their Will.
  3. Two Independent Witnesses: The Will must be attested by at least two independent adult witnesses who personally saw the testator sign.

The Legal Reality: Nowhere does the Indian Succession Act 1925 require an advocate's signature, a law firm letterhead, or a notary stamp. An Indian civil court judges a Will solely on whether Section 63 was followed, NOT on who typed the document.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Does an online Will require a lawyer's signature or stamp?

No. Indian law does not require any advocate or notary stamp on a Will. A Will is executed solely between the testator and two independent witnesses. Adding a lawyer's signature confers zero additional legal validity.

2. What if my bank or housing society challenges the online Will?

Banks and housing societies cannot reject a validly executed Will merely because it was drafted online. Under RBI Master Directions and the Indian Succession Act, an unprivileged Will fulfilling Section 63 criteria is a legally binding testamentary document.

3. Is my personal financial information kept safe?

Yes. Unlike traditional law offices where clerks and paralegals handle your confidential financial assets, SmartWill uses modern client-side encryption and adheres strictly to the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023.

4. Can I update my Will if I purchase a new property later?

Yes. Under Section 62 of the Indian Succession Act, a Will is always revocable during the testator's lifetime. You can simply generate an updated Will whenever your financial or family situation evolves.
