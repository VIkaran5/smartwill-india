Is Will Registration Mandatory in Bangalore?

No, registration is 100% optional.

Under Section 18(e) of the Indian Registration Act, 1908, testamentary documents (Wills) do not require mandatory registration in the state of Karnataka.

Supreme Court Precedent: A Will drafted on plain A4 white paper and duly signed by the testator in the presence of two independent witnesses is 100% legally enforceable in Indian civil courts under Section 63 of the Indian Succession Act 1925. Visiting a Sub-Registrar Office in Bangalore is completely optional.

When Should Bangalore Residents Register at an SRO?

While optional, registering your Will at a Bangalore SRO is advantageous in specific situations:

  • Multiple Properties or High-Value Estates: If you own multiple properties across Bengaluru Urban and Rural districts (e.g., plots in Devanahalli and flats in Marathahalli), SRO registration creates an undisputed public record.
  • Preventing Family Challenges: If you are distributing assets unequally among children or bequeathing to a spouse exclusively, SRO registration proves you were of sound mind without coercion.
  • Official Archival in Book 3: SRO records are permanent. Even if your physical paper copy is lost, your beneficiaries can retrieve a certified copy from the Karnataka Registration Department.

True Government Fees in Karnataka (Under ₹500)

Official Karnataka government fees for Will registration are minimal:

  • Stamp Duty: ₹0 (Exempt under Indian Stamp Act 1899).
  • Registration Fee: ₹200 flat government fee.
  • Scanning & User Charges: ₹100 to ₹250 (charged per page for Kaveri 2.0 digital scanning).
  • Total Official Outlay: Under ₹500.

How to Use Karnataka's Kaveri 2.0 Portal for SRO Appointments

  1. Generate Your Document: Create your legally validated Will on SmartWill India for ₹299. Print two original copies on plain A4 paper.
  2. Book Slot on Kaveri 2.0: Visit kaverionline.karnataka.gov.in, select "Pre-Registration Data Entry" or appointment booking, and choose your local Bangalore SRO.
  3. Assemble Identification Documents:
    • Testator original Aadhaar card + PAN card + passport size photographs
    • Two independent witnesses with original Aadhaar cards (Note: Witnesses must NOT be beneficiaries under Section 67!)
    • Medical fitness certificate from a registered MBBS doctor if the testator is 65+ years old
  4. Appear at the Sub-Registrar Office: The Sub-Registrar interviews the testator to ensure sound mind and free will, takes biometric fingerprint scans, and captures web-camera photographs.
  5. Receipt & Numbering: The SRO applies the official endorsement and registers the document into Book 3.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How do tech workers distribute RSUs / ESOPs in a Will?

Vested company shares, RSUs, and foreign brokerage accounts (such as Morgan Stanley, Charles Schwab, or Vested) can be enumerated under movable assets in your SmartWill, specifying the percentage allocation to your spouse or children.

2. What happens to Bangalore apartment ownership after demise?

When you have a legally valid Will, your beneficiaries can directly submit the Will, death certificate, and Khata transfer application to the Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP) or BDA for Khata transfer without fighting over succession certificates.

3. Can an IT professional execute a Will online while working from home?

Yes. The entire drafting and generation process on SmartWill India takes just 10 minutes from your laptop. You then print it on plain paper and sign with two adult witnesses (such as colleagues, neighbors, or friends).

4. Can I appoint my spouse as both Executor and Beneficiary?

Yes. Indian law allows an Executor to also be a beneficiary. However, they must NOT sign as one of the two attesting witnesses due to Section 67 of the Indian Succession Act.
